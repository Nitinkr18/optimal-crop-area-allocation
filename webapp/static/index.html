Optimal Crop-Area Allocation for Agricultural Resource Planning

A linear-programming approach using district-level crop production data from the Government of India.

Nitin Krishna V · Vishal Suresh · Manogna Challa · Gaurav Mahesh — Amrita School of AI, Coimbatore

Problem

Land is fixed and yields differ several-fold between crops, yet land is allocated by habit, not by comparing the yield records that already exist.

Approach

Two linear programs whose every coefficient (yield, minimum and maximum area) comes from the government crop record.

Scope

One state and one season, yields averaged over the available years. No price data exists, so we optimise output and land, not profit.

Questions

Q1

Maximise output. With a fixed land budget, how many hectares per crop give the most total production?

Q2

Minimise land. To produce at least the historical output, what is the smallest area needed?

    How the solution works

    Every crop starts at its lower area limit. Remaining land goes to the highest-yield crop first, up to its upper limit, then the next. The crop where land runs out sits between its limits, and its yield is the value of one more hectare (the shadow price λ*). A pulse floor forces some land to low-yield pulses first, so the gain shrinks.

    Assumptions

    • Only crops measured by weight are pooled (no nuts or bales).
    • Yields are averages over 1998–2019; weather is not modelled.
    • Area limits come from each crop's own history.
    • Prices and costs are not in the data, so the objective is output, not profit.
